Frequently asked questions

Are you accepting new patients?

Yes. We welcome new patients ages 7 and up. Book online or call (817) 866-2065.

What insurance do you accept?

We're in network with Cigna, Delta Dental, Ameritas, Assurant/DHA, Guardian, Humana and Principal, and we accept many other plans out of network. Call us to check your plan.

What are your hours?

Monday through Thursday, 8:00 AM to 4:00 PM, closed for lunch from 12 to 1. Closed Friday through Sunday.

Why should I visit the dentist regularly?

Regular visits catch problems early, when they're smaller and cost less to fix. Most people should come in twice a year.

Why do I need to floss?

Brushing cleans the surfaces of your teeth, but it can't reach between them or below the gumline. Flossing removes the plaque and bacteria hiding there.

How often should I brush and floss?

Brush at least twice a day, especially before bed, with a soft-bristled brush and fluoride toothpaste. Floss once a day.

How safe are dental X-rays?

Very safe. Digital X-rays use a very small amount of radiation, and we take them only when they help your care.

I'm nervous about the dentist. What can I do?

Tell us. We'll go at your pace, explain each step, and can offer nitrous oxide or oral sedation to help you relax.

When should my child first see a dentist?

The American Academy of Pediatric Dentistry recommends a first visit about six months after the first tooth comes in, or by the first birthday. We see patients ages 7 and up.

Why do baby teeth matter?

Baby teeth help kids chew, speak clearly and hold space for adult teeth. Taking care of them helps adult teeth come in straight and healthy.

When should my child have an orthodontic check?

The American Association of Orthodontists recommends a first orthodontic screening by age 7, when problems are easier to spot.

Am I too old to straighten my teeth?

No. As long as your teeth and gums are healthy, adults can get orthodontic treatment, including clear aligners like Invisalign.

I've lost a tooth. What are my options?

Dental implants, bridges and complete or partial dentures can all replace missing teeth. We'll go over the pros and cons for you.

What is a root canal?

A root canal removes infected tissue from inside a tooth, then cleans and seals it so you can keep the tooth. With good numbing, it usually feels like getting a filling.

What is gum disease?

Gum (periodontal) disease is an infection of the gums and the bone that holds your teeth. It starts as gingivitis and can lead to tooth loss if it isn't treated.

What are the signs of gum disease?

Red, swollen or bleeding gums, gums pulling away from your teeth, loose teeth, bad breath that won't go away, or a change in your bite. Call us if you notice any of these.

What can I do about bad breath?

Brush, floss, drink plenty of water and avoid tobacco. Cavities, gum disease, dry mouth or a poorly fitting appliance can also cause it, so come see us. If it continues, check with your doctor.
